# Pulse — Competitive Landscape & Differentiation

## The Real Competitor

**Notion / Google Sheets.** That's what groups default to today. Beating that is job one.

---

## Partiful

Partiful = event RSVP + hype. Single event, known date, known venue. "Who's coming."

Pulse = group trip decision engine. No fixed date yet, no venue yet — the *planning* is the product. "Who wants what, who travels together, when do we go."

### Where they overlap
- Guest list
- Shared event context
- Mobile-first

### Where they immediately diverge

| Dimension | Partiful | Pulse |
|---|---|---|
| **Core Identity** | Guest List Tool | **Harmony Engine** |
| **Decision state** | Decision already made | Decision is the product |
| **Decision Model** | Majority Vote (Polls) | **Algorithmic Optimization (Matrix)** |
| **Outcome** | "Winner takes all" | **Sub-group formation (The Crew)** |
| **Social Goal** | Hype & Attendance | **Harmony & Logistics** |
| **Planning horizon** | Days/weeks out | Weeks/months out |
| **Friction** | Near zero (RSVP in 2s) | High — complexity is expected |
| **Core Question** | "Who's coming?" | "Who should do what together?" |

### The "Harmony Engine" Strategy
Partiful wins on *speed*; Pulse wins on *effectiveness*.
- **The Politeness Barrier:** Traditional polls force a "Yes/No" that people feel guilty about. The MUST/WANT/MEH matrix acts as a "Meh-diator," allowing users to signal preferences without killing the vibe.
- **Sub-Groups as a Feature:** In Partiful, if you don't attend the event, you've "failed" the social contract. In Pulse, splitting into sub-groups (e.g., 4 to the museum, 2 to the beach) is a success of the planning algorithm.
- **Algorithmic Logistics:** Beyond simple voting, Pulse identifies the "Maximum Overlap" for group activities and handles the transition between stops (multi-day logistics).

## Pulse's Moat

**1. Group compatibility as a named category.**
Nobody has named this space yet. "Who should do what together" ≠ "who's coming." Own the framing of "Harmony-as-a-Service."

**2. The Crew View (Sub-Group Formation).**
Building for the *split* rather than the *whole*. Pulse's data model assumes the group is dynamic, not a monolith. This is technically and psychologically difficult for itinerary-first (Wanderlog) or invite-first (Partiful) apps to retro-fit.

**3. Decision-First Architecture.**
Every feature is filtered through one lens: *"Does this help the group decide what to do Tuesday at 4 PM?"* This prevents feature creep into social feeds or photo albums.

**4. ***REMOVED*** revenue changes incentives.**
Pulse can stay free forever. If a large player launches a competing product and charges, Pulse undercuts on price while matching on features.

**5. Complexity tolerance.**
Pulse users *expect* to spend 20 minutes. Depth is the product. Partiful cannot serve this user without a full reposition.

## Real Threats

- **Google / Airbnb / TripAdvisor** — distribution advantage. Defense: own "group compatibility matrix" in users' minds + build ***REMOVED*** relationships before they enter.
- **Partiful expanding into travel** — possible but foreign to their brand DNA. Monitor.

## Go-To-Market Wedge

**Wedding-adjacent trips** are the strongest first-user wedge:
- **High-Yield Acquisition:** One organizer (MOH/Best Man) onboard 10–50 users in a single session.
- **The "Flying In" Problem:** Solves the 3-day logistical gap around the wedding day that wedding websites (The Knot) don't touch.
- **The Politeness Barrier:** Solves the awkwardness of mixed groups (college friends vs. cousins) by using anonymous MUST/WANT ratings.
- **Viral "Aha!" Moment:** Users realize they can split off into sub-groups (The Crew) based on shared interests without offending the larger group.
- **Network Effect:** A wedding guest is a high-probability future organizer for their own smaller friend trips.

**Secondary wedges (in order):**
1. Bachelorette/bachelor trips — high excitement, organizer-driven, high frequency.
2. Family reunion trips — high complexity, cross-generational, recurring annually.
3. Friend group international trips — the "Italy" use case; high stakes, high planning need.
4. Work offsites — high utility and willingness to pay, but higher barrier to entry.

### Acquisition strategy for the Wedding Wedge
- **Communities:** Target Reddit (`r/weddingplanning`), Facebook Groups, and Discord communities.
- **Positioning:** "The tool for the Friday-Sunday logistics your wedding website forgot."
- **Low Friction:** Onboarding must support "No-Account" rating so guests can contribute in < 60s.
- **Templates:** Provide pre-built "Wedding Weekend" templates (Brunch, Sightseeing, Pre-Drinks).
